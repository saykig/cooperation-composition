# Cooperation & Composition

This repository records the development of my MA major thesis: the questions, proofs, counterexamples, literature notes, computational experiments, failed directions, and changes in argument as the research develops.

There is already substantial work on cooperation, information design, signaling, incentives, enforcement, and strategic communication. I am not trying to rebuild those literatures. I am interested in a place where they seem to remain somewhat fragmented: **what happens to cooperation when different information sources and strategic actors are connected, and when institutions can respond by changing either who learns what or how strongly cooperation is enforced?**

This is an abstract mathematical question, but the structure appears in real institutions. Ceasefire and peacekeeping arrangements depend on monitoring, information access, and credible responses to violations. Security partnerships similarly depend on who shares intelligence with whom and what commitments accompany that information. Recent examples include [Reuters reporting on the transition from UN peacekeeping in Lebanon](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/un-peacekeeping-chief-lebanon-says-smooth-transition-is-critical-before-pull-out-2026-09-19/) and [Reuters reporting on U.S.–Saudi intelligence-sharing and targeting assistance](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/saudi-crown-prince-us-centcom-chief-discuss-developments-after-houthi-escalation-2026-09-14/).

## Current evolving research question

> How do connected information structures determine the incentives or enforcement required to sustain cooperation?

## Current working title

> **Composing Cooperation: Information Structure, Incentives, and Enforcement**

The question is intentionally allowed to change. The purpose of this repository is to preserve that research trajectory rather than make the project look more settled than it is.
